SAM'S TIKTOK ALGORITHM & CONTENT PLAYBOOK
A machine-readable skill spec for content creation agents
Core belief the whole system rests on: Stop trying to "game" the algorithm. The algorithm is not a slot machine you trick, it's a distribution system that reads human behaviour. Your job is to make content that earns watch time and deep engagement, then let distribution follow. Every rule below is downstream of that one idea.
________________


1. THE ALGORITHM MODEL — how the machine actually decides
* TikTok is a prediction engine, not a gatekeeper. It shows your video to a small test audience and predicts whether the next person will keep watching. Everything is a bet on retention.
* Distribution is behaviour-driven, not follower-driven. Existing followers are only an initial seed. A video's real reach is decided by how strangers respond, so treat every upload as a first impression with a cold audience.
* Ranked by engagement depth, not volume. The hierarchy of signals your agent should optimise for, strongest to weakest:
   1. Completion rate (did they finish)
   2. Rewatches / loops (did they watch again — the strongest single signal)
   3. Saves (will they want this later — highest-intent signal)
   4. Shares (would they send this to someone — social currency)
   5. Comments (did it provoke a reaction)
   6. Likes (cheapest signal, weakest evidence of value)
* Watch time is the currency. A 30-second video watched to the end beats a 3-minute video abandoned at 8 seconds. Never buy length you can't hold.
* Views are rented, retention is owned. A viral spike means nothing if the retention graph is a cliff; that pattern trains the system that your content doesn't satisfy.
* Every video is a fresh audition. Past performance gives you a bigger initial test pool, not a guaranteed outcome. Never blame the algorithm for a retention problem.
* The algorithm rewards specificity. One clear topic per video tells the system exactly who to show it to. Vague content gets shown to nobody in particular.
________________


2. THE HOOK — the first 1–3 seconds
* The hook is a promise, and the video is the receipt. If the payoff doesn't match the promise, you win the open and lose the completion.
* Hook types that work (give the agent these as a menu, not a formula to overuse):
   * Bold claim — a specific, falsifiable statement
   * Direct question the viewer is already asking themselves
   * Surprising result shown before explanation (result-first, then the how)
   * Clear problem named in the viewer's own words
   * Pattern interrupt — visual or tonal break from what they expected
   * Contrarian correction — "everyone tells you X, that's why yours flop"
* No throat-clearing. No "hey guys," no logo intros, no "in this video I'm going to." The first frame is the hook.
* The hook and the payoff must be in the same room. If the first line is about hooks and the payoff is about hashtags, completion dies at the seam.
* On-screen text must carry the hook too. Most viewers watch muted in the first second; if the text doesn't land, the audio never mattered.
* One idea per video. Two ideas halves both completion rates. If the agent has two insights, that's two videos, not one longer one.
________________


3. TIKTOK SEO — being found, not just fed
* TikTok is a search engine with a video player attached. A meaningful share of views now come from search, and search views are the highest-intent views you'll ever get.
* Say the target phrase out loud. Spoken audio is transcribed and indexed. If the phrase never leaves your mouth, it doesn't exist to search.
* Put the exact phrase in on-screen text, early and legible.
* Front-load the caption with the searchable phrase — the first few words matter more than the rest.
* Use 3–5 specific hashtags, not 20 generic ones. Precise topic beats broad vanity tags. #contentstrategy outperforms #fyp.
* Repeat keyword variations naturally across speech, text, caption — natural language, never keyword stuffing.
* Research demand before creating. Use Creator Search Insights to see what your audience searches and what the platform already associates your account with. Don't guess demand, read it.
* Answer one specific query per video. Search intent is narrow; a video that answers "how to film a hook" should not also be about lighting.
* Add a location tag when the content serves a local audience. Free relevance signal.
* Title the video like a search result, not like an artwork.
________________


4. RETENTION & STRUCTURE — holding the 3 seconds after the hook
* Duration is a target, not a default. Cut the video to the shortest length that delivers the full payoff. Delete every frame that doesn't add information, tension, or payoff.
* Pattern interrupt every 3–5 seconds: cut, zoom, angle change, text overlay, prop, sound shift. Attention decays predictably, refill it before it empties.
* Open a loop, close a loop. Tease the payoff early ("the third one is the one nobody does"), pay it off at the end. That's what makes people stay.
* Build the loop. End on a line that makes the first frame make sense on a rewatch, or that hands off to the next video. Rewatches are the strongest signal available to you.
* Payoff placement: deliver real value before the final third, then use the closing seconds for the CTA. Never promise value only at the very end.
* Kill dead air. Silence, pauses, "um," and slow intros all read as drop-off points.
* Front-load value, back-load ask. Teach first, sell last.
* Read the retention graph honestly. Find the exact second viewers leave and fix that second in the next video — that's where the hook lives, the boring bit lives, or the promise broke.
________________


5. GOOD CONTENT — the thing that actually travels
* Valuable enough that someone would save it. Saves are the clearest vote of confidence the platform has.
* Specific beats clever. "Post at 7pm" is advice; "post your first video at 7pm on a Tuesday, and here's why" is content.
* Show the working. Reveal the mechanism, not just the conclusion — people share explanations, not announcements.
* Use trends as a format, not as a substitute for an idea. The sound is the container. Your insight is the thing inside it. Empty containers don't travel.
* Rewatchable > impressive. A video worth seeing twice beats a video that's merely well-produced once.
* Be the source, not the aggregator. Original framing is what earns shares and follows; reposted information does not.
* Every video should be able to answer: who is this for, what do they get, why should they care in the first second?
________________


6. WORLD BUILDING & SIGNATURE STYLE — why people follow rather than watch
* People follow a person with a point of view, not a feed of tips. Define the belief your content keeps proving.
* World building = recurring places, phrases, characters, formats, and running jokes that make a new viewer feel they've walked into something already in motion.
* Signature style is repetition with intention. Same opening energy, same visual grammar, same editing rhythm — so a viewer recognises you with the sound off.
* Niche discipline beats topic drift. A consistent audience is built by being predictably about one thing. The algorithm also learns who to send you to.
* Series > one-offs. Named, numbered, recurring formats compound: they create return viewers and tell the platform what to expect.
* Personality is a retention mechanism. Flat delivery is a drop-off point; your voice is part of the structure.
________________


7. THE AGENT OPERATING RULES — encode these as hard constraints
* Never write a video without stating the one idea it delivers.
* Never write a hook that the script doesn't pay off within 20 seconds.
* Never exceed the shortest length that delivers the payoff. Recommend a duration explicitly.
* Never produce a vague niche-less script. Name the audience and the search query it answers.
* Always output: hook (verbatim first line), on-screen text for the first frame, the single idea, the payoff line, the loop/close, the CTA phrased as intent ("save this for when you film"), 3–5 specific hashtags, and a suggested length.
* Always include one pattern interrupt marker per 3–5 seconds in the script's beat column.
* Reject your own output if: the hook and payoff mismatch, there are two ideas, the CTA asks before it gives, or the hashtags are generic.
* When reviewing performance, diagnose in this order: retention graph → hook → pacing → promise/payoff match → metadata. Never start with "the algorithm."
* Never recommend engagement bait, follow-for-follow, or buying views. It corrupts the signal and teaches the system the wrong audience.
________________


8. POST-MORTEM LOOP — what the agent does after every video
* Log the retention curve and name the drop-off second.
* Classify each video: won the hook / lost the hook, held the middle / died in the middle, stuck the landing / lost the end.
* One variable per test. Change the hook, or the length, or the format — never all three, or you learn nothing.
* Reward rate over reach. A 12% completion on 900 views is a better teacher than 3% on 90,000.
* Rebuild winners, don't just celebrate them. Turn the best-performing structure into a repeatable series template.
* Retire formats on a three-strike rule: if a structure loses retention three times, kill it rather than blaming the topic.

14. Pre-publish checklist for agents
* Is it clear which Tok or niche and which content pillar this belongs to?
* Does it use one of the brand's recurring formats or signature cues?
* Does the hook land in the first 1–3 seconds, both spoken and on screen?
* Is the topic clear early?
* Is the payoff held until about 10–12 seconds, with no dead time?
* Is the target keyword said out loud, shown on screen and used in the caption?
* Are there 3–5 specific hashtags and no generic ones?
* Is a location tag added if local reach matters?
* Does it entertain or educate, rather than just sell?
* Does it feel native and human rather than polished like an ad?
* Is there a clear reason to comment, share or rewatch?
* Does it build the brand's world, or is it a shuffle post?
