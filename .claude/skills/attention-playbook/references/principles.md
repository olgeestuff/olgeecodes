# Principles (platform-agnostic)

## 1. Entertain the masses, educate the interested, sell to the committed (Adley)
- People open the app to be distracted, not to learn about your service. **Meet them in entertainment first; that earns the right to educate.**
- Educational content is not a strategy on its own. Unless it's packaged as entertainment, people scroll before they learn.
- Big brands disguise education in entertainment: micro-dramas, series, characters.
- **On-screen text is premium real estate.** Use it to pre-frame how the viewer should watch.
- **Every hook must** (1) create an emotion and (2) leave an unanswered question.
- Value alone isn't enough. People need a reason to *care*, not just a reason to agree.
- A viral post isn't automatically a brand-building post. Reach must quickly tie back to the North Star positioning.
- Be broad enough to earn distribution and specific enough to qualify the right buyer. Don't talk industry-speak before you've earned attention.
- A great premise survives imperfect footage. Great footage can't rescue a boring premise.
- **Consistency plus experimentation,** or you plateau.
- **Buyers with money care about time and efficiency.** Frame the offer as hours saved, mistakes avoided and things that no longer slip.
- **STD idea grid:** list 10 **S**truggles, 10 **T**opics and 10 **D**esires, then mix and match into hundreds of ideas.
- **90% rule:** aim for 9 in 10 viewers still watching at 6 seconds. That puts you in the top tier of what the algorithm serves.

## 2. Health, wealth, relationships (Russell Brunson)
Every piece, even a joke or a tune, should touch one core desire:
- **Wealth:** money, time saved, cost avoided, efficiency, growth.
- **Health:** stress, overwhelm, sleep, peace of mind, not carrying everything in your head.
- **Relationships:** tenants, staff, contractors, clients, family time, trust.

## 3. Levels of awareness (Eugene Schwartz)
Match the message to where the viewer is. Say less, but say the right thing.

| Level | They know… | Lead with |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | nothing about the problem | a story, character or situation they recognise |
| Problem-aware | the pain | the pain in their words, plus "you're not alone" |
| Solution-aware | that fixes exist | why *this kind* of fix (a simple app on Google tools) |
| Product-aware | Sholz's offer | proof, demos, cases |
| Most aware | they want it | the offer and the CTA |

Cold feeds (TikTok FYP) are mostly unaware or problem-aware.

## 4. Proof beats adjectives (Ogilvy)
"At 60 miles an hour, the loudest sound in this car is the clock."
- Don't say fast. Say **"saves two hours"**.
- Don't say trusted. Say **who trusted it**.
- Don't say durable. Show **what it survived**.
- Banned words without proof: premium, luxury, high quality, best-in-class, seamless, powerful, revolutionary.

## 5. The 11 Laws of Content Creation (via mila.sokolskiy)
1. **Mirror:** people follow you because they see themselves in you.
2. **Future self:** you prove the life they want is possible.
3. **Messaging:** one clear message that every video ties back to.
4. **Enemy:** give the audience a common enemy (paperwork, spreadsheet chaos, overpriced software, "it's in my head").
5. **Before:** your old self is your best marketing tool.
6. **Consistency:** be consistent with the version of yourself you want to be known for.
7. **Polarity:** say one thing your industry disagrees with.
8. **Transformation:** show the process *and* the result.
9. **Levels:** the first videos are meant to be bad. Get through them.
10. **Gap:** make them feel the gap between where they are and where they want to be.
11. **Repeated offender:** say one thing 100 different ways.

## 6. Game theory and mechanism design (Sholz)
- **Game theory** predicts behaviour from the rules. Platform rule: content that keeps strangers watching gets more strangers.
- **Mechanism design** chooses the rules *we* control (hook, text, pacing, loop, caption, posting time, series format) so the behaviour we want (watch, rewatch, save, share, comment) is the natural response.
- People respond predictably to curiosity gaps, identity ("that's me"), enemies, status and social currency (sharing makes them look smart or funny). Design for those responses, and stay truthful.

## 7. World building and series
- Recurring characters and roles (e.g. "the typical US landlord", the overwhelmed property manager, Sholz as the fixer), recurring phrases and named numbered series compound recognition.
- Open question for Sholz: keep *characters* consistent or keep *roles* consistent? Default for now: consistent **roles**, flexible casting.
